## High

**H1. A lost worker can hang every participant waiting for wave completion.** `src/pool.rs:126–145,165–180`, `Pool::new` worker closure and `Pool::run`; `src/workspace.rs:23–30`, `WorkerScratch::new`.

- **Reach:** internal panic on a background thread, either during scratch initialization or after accepting a wave. This is a failure-containment defect; I did not find a malformed signature that triggers a worker panic.
- **Argument:** `remaining_workers` counts *configured threads*, not workers known to have started or tasks known to have completed. The decrement is only on the normal path after `drain`. A panic before the decrement terminates the detached worker, not the process. `done.wait()` then has a predicate that cannot become true. This holds even if the caller has already computed every task. A panic after claiming a task also leaves that output at its default value, so just moving the decrement into a destructor would not suffice.
- **Fix:** make startup and wave failure explicit. A worker must report either successful completion or failure; a failed wave must not publish its outputs. Notify waiters on failure and retire/rebuild the pool, propagate the panic on the caller, or explicitly abort the process if that is the intended fail-stop policy. Add injected startup/task failures with a subprocess watchdog.

**H2. Partial pool construction leaks live threads; retry is not recovery.** `src/pool.rs:117–150,184–187`, `Pool::new`, `pool`.

- **Reach:** documented `SigningKey::from_seed` on a multicore host where the OS permits some, but not all, of the requested thread creations. A process may catch the resulting panic. The BLINCS pool is in addition to whatever resources the hash runtime initialized.
- **Argument:** successful `JoinHandle`s are discarded immediately. If a later `.spawn(...).expect(...)` fails, each successful worker owns an `Arc` of the now-unpublished state and waits on it forever. There is no shutdown generation. `OnceLock` does not publish a failed initialization, so a later attempt can construct a *different* pool; it cannot reclaim the first one. A 128-core host requests 64 background workers for 64 tasks, in addition to the participating caller.
- **Fix:** construct a usable pool with the number of successful starts, including zero, or keep handles and implement shutdown/join on failed construction. Use a startup acknowledgement if scratch initialization may fail. Cap useful background parallelism at `tasks - 1`. Test failure after a specified number of successful spawns, not just failure of the first spawn.

**H3. Reinitializing randomness does not make BLINCS safe in a fork child.** `SECURITY.md:18`, `SPEC.md:226`, `src/randomness.rs:86–89`, fork contract; `src/pool.rs:153–180,184–187`, `Pool::run`, `pool`.

- **Reach:** an application follows the documented guidance after forking a multithreaded process that has constructed a signing key.
- **Argument:** a fork child inherits the initialized `OnceLock`, mutex/condvar state and `n_workers`, but not the background threads. Even in the favorable case where all locks were free, the next dispatched wave can wait for acknowledgements from nonexistent workers. `Randomness::initialize` replaces none of this state. There is also the more fundamental POSIX restriction on what a multithreaded child may do before `exec`; Rust allocation and hash-runtime initialization are not a post-fork recovery protocol.
- **Fix:** require `fork` **followed by `exec`** before using BLINCS in such a child, or start independent signer processes before initializing threaded runtimes. Separate this from restoring a complete VM image, where the draw sequence, rather than necessarily the absence of workers, is the issue. Do not promise an in-process post-fork reset of just the RNG.

**H4. The advertised 133.2 bits are a FORS event estimate, not the security level of this signature scheme.** `SPEC.md:5,132–135,188,235–237`; `DESIGN.md:29–40,84–101,120`; `src/few_time.rs:8–18`, `parse_digest`; `src/lib.rs:177–184`, `verify_short`.

- **Reach:** ordinary single-key use at the advertised budget; this concerns the security claim, not a parsing bug.
- **Argument:** verification ultimately compares a **128-bit** root. Even in an idealized random-function analysis, there is a generic target-match term on that scale; a \(2^{-133.2}\) cover probability does not eliminate it. The displayed FORS formula is appropriate for the stated *single fresh query*, with independent uniform honest selections: condition on \(r\) hits at its address, compute the coverage of one index, then take the 16th power. It is not a bound on all ways of passing verification. Also, verification consumes **193** bits of `D`, not 256. Two digests differing only in the unused 63 bits select identical coordinates. For orientation, matching any of \(q\) independently fixed coordinate strings has scale \(q/2^{193}\) per independent trial, not \(q/2^{256}\); an arbitrary pair collision is a different event again. Neither observation by itself is a practical forgery.
- **Fix:** say “128-bit classical design target; estimated FORS cover exponent 133.2 at \(q=2^{32}\).” Use that distinction in the headline and comparison tables. Define \(a=10\) in the formula. Remove the claim that retaining all 256 bits prevents coordinate collisions. State the single-key, query, signing-budget and ideal-hash assumptions; don't present these numbers as a multi-user or quantum bound.

## Medium

**M1. A caller-side unwind can retain a signing secret in the global pool and silently disable parallel dispatch.** `src/pool.rs:27–40,160–180`, `JobKind`, `Pool::run`; `src/workspace.rs:47–57`, `with_caller_scratch`, `with_verify_workspace`.

- **Reach:** a panic in caller scratch access or computation after the wave is published. One safe application context worth distinguishing from an ordinary `Read` callback is a user TLS destructor calling BLINCS after BLINCS's own TLS value has been destroyed.
- **Argument:** `LocalKey::with` can panic on access to destroyed TLS. If this happens at line 174, `active = None` is skipped and `dispatch` can be poisoned. `try_lock()` conflates `Poisoned` with `WouldBlock`; subsequent calls run synchronously rather than repairing or reporting the abandoned pool. `active` can retain the job's **copy of `SecretKey`**, not just scratch intermediates, for the rest of the process. Independent of the pool, verification also uses unconditional TLS `.with`; a panic escaping a TLS destructor can abort the process.
- **Fix:** acquire usable caller scratch before publishing work; distinguish contention from poison; guarantee cleanup only after outstanding workers are accounted for. Use `try_with` with an owned temporary-scratch fallback or explicitly exclude TLS teardown from the API contract. Exercise teardown order and caught caller panic separately from caught reader panic.

- **Reach:** move/share a constructed signing key and `Randomness` to a fresh application thread; also every newly created BLINCS background worker.
- **Argument:** BLINCS describes `ensure_init` as preparing **the calling thread**. On the new application thread, `draw` and `derive_salt` hash before `content_from_*` calls `ensure_init`. On the background thread, scratch is constructed and `TreeHasher` used without any visible thread preparation. The global runtime was initialized during construction, but that does not prove a per-thread precondition. Conversely, if the upstream contract is process-global and these particular operations require no caller preparation, this is a documentation/test issue, not a demonstrated runtime bug.
- **Fix:** check the **0.10.0** contracts separately for convenience hashes, `Batch`, `TreeHasher::on_this_core()` and full-width hashing. Test public `sign` with an existing RNG on a fresh thread; `src/test.rs:348–364` uses the internal fixed-draw entry instead.

**M3. The `Pipeline` room confidentiality boundary is unproved, and the snooping test never inspects a room.** `src/content.rs:66–91`, `content_from_reader`; `src/test.rs:535–582`, `reader_never_sees_previous_callers_bytes`; `SPEC.md:240`, `SECURITY.md:19`.

- **Reach:** public safe `Read` implementations can inspect the entire `&mut [u8]` passed to them, including bytes they do not report having written.
- **Argument:** BLINCS zeroes `first`. It does **not** initialize a newly acquired room before exposing its suffix, except for the prefix copied into the first room. Fresh `Pipeline` identity does not establish fresh or scrubbed backing storage. Also distinguish *cross-call* reuse from recycling rooms holding an earlier part of the *same* stream, which matters if a reader delegates to different producers. Safe initialized bytes are not necessarily confidentially blank bytes. The test's second message is six bytes, and its inspection is conditional on `pos == 0`: it establishes nothing about pipeline rooms. **No actual leak across two pipelines is established by the supplied source.**
- **Fix:** either cite and pin a sufficient upstream storage-isolation contract, or initialize the exposed portion of each acquired room once before the callback can see it. If stronger separation between producers is required, define that boundary too. Test long successive calls that inspect newly acquired buffers *after* 16 KiB, with enough data for room recycling, following success, error, and unwind.

**M4. Stream length, terminal events, and unwind behavior are delegated contracts.** `src/content.rs:28–35,67–72,93–117`, `content_from_slice`, `content_from_reader`; `SPEC.md:9,115`.

- **Reach:** unknown-length streams, errors or panics while a room/message is live, and, as a specification limit, a stream exceeding `u64::MAX` bytes.
- **Argument:** the large-slice path supplies its exact checked `u64` length and updates once. The reader supplies `None` and never counts the aggregate length in BLINCS. It cannot itself enforce the specified maximum or return a defined oversized-stream error. On normal end the loop discards any event other than `Hashed`; on abort it accepts the first event of any kind; on unwind it performs neither explicit terminal sequence. With one message these may all be correct **if** the upstream event, `ready`, and drop contracts guarantee them. For example, ignoring a non-hash terminal event *if one is possible after end* would leave the code waiting for a hash that cannot arrive. This is not evidence that such an event exists.
- **Fix:** use checked cumulative length with a tested overflow helper and a defined rejection path. Record the permitted terminal states for a single message, handle them explicitly, and verify drop/unwind with outstanding committed work and an uncommitted room. Size those tests relative to `ROOM_LEN`, not just fixed 40,000- or 90,000-byte prefixes. Test re-entry from the room path too; `src/test.rs:586–614` re-enters from the stack path.

**M5. Signing self-verification doesn't bind the serialized salt back to `short`.** `src/lib.rs:165–174,234–245,284–286`, `VerifyingKey::verify_short`, `SigningKey::finish_signature`; `QUALITY.md:17`, signing self-check.

- **Reach:** internal caller supplies a mismatched `salt` and `short`, or an assembly fault corrupts only `sig[..16]`. Current normal public callers supply a matching pair.
- **Argument:** `verify_short` takes `R` from the signature but takes `e` from the supplied `short`; it never reads `signature.salt()`. Thus the internal check can pass for a serialized signature that public `verify` rejects. This needs neither a reread of an external stream nor a symmetric fault in signing and verifying.
- **Fix:** give `finish_signature` a single typed short-content object; derive the serialized salt from it. Independently check `signature.salt() == short[..N]` as part of `verify_short` when it is used to check assembly. Plant a fault solely in the serialized salt and require the signer to reject its output.

**M6. The randomness-file update is serialized, not transactional; creation errors have ambiguous filesystem outcomes.** `src/randomness.rs:115–133,145–158`, `Randomness::create`, `Randomness::initialize`; `DESIGN.md:184–187`; `QUALITY.md:14`.

- **Reach:** a partial write followed by an I/O error, process death during creation, power loss during rotation, or failure to open/sync the parent directory. All can occur to a compliant caller.
- **Argument:** `write_all` is not an atomic 40-byte record replacement. On error, or after a crash, there may be a mixed counter/seed record. Length and magic do not detect such a record. Crash recovery is not limited to “old state reverted” versus “new state persisted.” Creation exposes the new name before writing finishes; unlink on write/file-sync failure is best effort. A parent-open or parent-sync error returns `Err` **leaving the completed file in place**, and retrying `create` then fails. This is primarily an availability/recovery issue; fresh OS input and the secret salt derivation still matter.
- **Fix:** specify the possible states after `Err` or crash and give a safe operator recovery procedure. Test injected short-write/error, file-sync and directory-sync failures. If atomic rotation is required, use a journal/two slots or atomic installation with a **stable, separate lock object**; simply renaming over the inode on which clients take locks breaks that locking design.

**M7. `create` can clean up or sync a different pathname from the one it created.** `src/randomness.rs:105–115,124–132`, `Randomness::create`.

- **Reach:** a relative path plus concurrent process-wide current-directory changes is sufficient; hostile replacement of path components is a further scenario outside the documented private-directory assumption.
- **Argument:** the file write uses an already opened handle, but `remove_file(path)` and `File::open(parent)` resolve the path again. Two different private directories are enough for the relative-path case. On the failure path, cleanup can remove a file other than the created file; on the success path the durability acknowledgement can concern the wrong directory. `create_new` correctly protects the *initial open*, not these later operations.
- **Fix:** anchor operations to a stable directory handle and stable entry ownership, or require a stable trusted directory and resolve relative paths once under that contract. Prefer leaving a documented failed provisioning artifact over unlinking a path whose identity cannot be established.

**M8. The native signing API is effectively Unix-specific.** `src/randomness.rs:25–28,105–115,128–132,139–151`, `read_os_randomness`, `Randomness::{create,initialize}`; `Cargo.toml:1–12`.

- **Reach:** documented startup on ordinary native Windows.
- **Argument:** `"/dev/urandom"` is a pathname, not a Windows CSPRNG API. Usually it will not exist; an ordinary file at the resolved path would not be a substitute. Directory opening/sync semantics and file protection also differ. Even if verification works, the public signer requires a `Randomness` which the supported constructors cannot ordinarily produce there.
- **Fix:** declare and test a Unix-only signing boundary, or use vetted platform entropy, file-protection and durability APIs on each supported platform. State the supported Rust version and target requirements, including availability of 64-bit atomics; “32-bit `usize`” and “has `AtomicU64`” are separate questions.

**M9. Several security and constant-work claims need narrower definitions.** `SPEC.md:123,225–231,238`; `DESIGN.md:23,118,171,180,187`; `src/one_time.rs:55–86,197–212,230–235`, `advance_chains`, `write_layer_signature`, `verify_layer`; `src/few_time.rs:55–56,71–82`, `build_fors_tree`.

- **Reach:** ordinary signatures, even with ideal randomness.
- **Argument:**
  - `R` does not **prevent WOTS address reuse**. At \(q=2^{32}\), the expected number of unordered pairs at the same 33-bit bottom address is about \(2^{30}\). What protects WOTS here is that a fixed position signs the **same** FORS root, or the same child subtree root at higher layers. The FORS disclosure set, not a new WOTS message at that position, changes. Secret `R` limits advance steering of the honest signer; it doesn't make address collisions impossible.
  - The atomic counter ensures distinct **inputs** within a run, not mathematically distinct PRF outputs or truncated salts. Even with a perfect run, random 128-bit salts have collision probability about \(2^{-65}\) over \(2^{32}\) signatures. A chance collision is not the same as being able to predict the next salt. Forks and live clones are other reasons the “only triple reset” statement is too strong.
  - No grinding is not constant latency. WOTS sorts by digit-dependent length, changes batch sizes, may exit early, and accesses values in digit-dependent order. FORS path selection is address-dependent. There is a useful nuance: for a correct Rust signature, signing plus self-verification performs \(d_c+(15-d_c)=15\) chain advances per signed chain. That does **not** fix the batch schedule, memory traffic, synchronization, or latency. The control data are derivable from the message and completed signature, not raw seed bytes or unopened chain values. They need not already be known to an observer while signing a private message.
  - Salted hashing here is a randomized **target-collision** argument requiring the submitter's target message to be fixed without advance knowledge of the salt. It is not an unconditional equivalence to every notion of second-preimage security. Likewise, hashing an exact length with appropriate BLAKE3 flags is not the claim that the set of input byte strings is prefix-free.
- **Fix:** document these actual invariants. Promise no rejection sampling, bounded chain lengths, and no *grinding-induced* latency tail. Do not promise global constant time, unique salts, or the impossibility of WOTS position reuse.

**M10. Positive controls can report success for infrastructure failures, not detection of the planted fault.** `tools/controls.py:123–127,138–156`, `run_nextest`, `main`; `tests/integration.rs:50–59`, Python rejection check.

- **Reach:** a control causes a build failure, its filter stops selecting the intended test, the runner fails, or the Python verifier raises an exception for a malformed case.
- **Argument:** *any* nonzero status is “CAUGHT”; there is no evidence check that the selected test ran and failed for the intended reason, and no timeout in `subprocess.run`. A green unmutated baseline doesn't rule out these outcomes on a mutated run. The integration test likewise treats a Python traceback as signature rejection. The controls edit the working tree in place; `finally` helps with ordinary exceptions but not a killed process or concurrent modifications.
- **Fix:** isolate controls in disposable worktrees; verify build success, exact selected test and failure result from structured output, and fail the gate separately on timeout/runner error. Require the Python CLI's defined `INVALID` result as well as its rejection status. Include controls for signer self-check and failed-wave handling.

**L2. “Textbook FIPS 205” should not imply FIPS encoding or proof equivalence.** `SPEC.md:5,61–67,133–135,165–175`; `DESIGN.md:84,118`; `src/one_time.rs:29–42`, `digits`; `reference/blincs.py:74–94`, `digits`, `parse_digest`.

For example, byte `0x12` gives BLINCS message digits `[2,1]`; FIPS `base_2b` takes the high-order digit first. BLINCS also puts the low checksum digit first and defines its own address format, FORS bit order and hash instantiation. **L10. The optimizer checks the adopted parameters but does not optimize the adopted protocol.** `tools/optimize.py:88–109,131–150,242–256,259–317,339–356,464–474,496,522–525`; `DESIGN.md:54–73,82–93`, frontier and floor claims.

- **Ordinary scheduling — no lost wakeup found.** `src/pool.rs:89–96,130–144,153–180`, `Job::drain`, `Pool::run`. Under normal completion, generation and completion predicates are tested under the matching mutex; a late-starting worker sees the changed generation. The next dispatched wave cannot supersede the current one before all acknowledgements. Relaxed `fetch_add` need only assign distinct task IDs; output publication has mutex synchronization. One core runs locally, and a contending caller drains its **own** job. This is a shared atomic task counter, not a deque-based work-stealing algorithm. No public API gives out a pool/TLS/file-lock guard that a caller could `mem::forget` to wedge this protocol. Forgetting an ordinary key can of course retain its memory. Reader callbacks occur before wave dispatch and outside the BLINCS scratch borrow; they are not the caller-side pool panic in M1.
- **Integer and hash-call dimensions — no current 32-bit indexing defect found.** `src/params.rs:31–40,261–282,296–310`; `src/workspace.rs:8–29,41–44`; `src/one_time.rs:98–115,163–184`; `src/content.rs:25–35,71–105`. Wide addresses stay in `u64`; narrowed leaves, levels, indices and task offsets fit their destinations with these constants. `MAX_BATCH=2240` covers both 64×35 chains and 1,024 FORS leaves; 512 pair slots divide the relevant larger power-of-two parent levels. `hash_many` input parts and output prefixes have matching counts, and the fixed message areas consumed are populated. Keyed versus derive-key `Mode` use matches SPEC. Slice `update_multithreaded` is given the same data length as the hasher hint. The remaining dependency sign-offs are **thread preparation**, **room initialization/isolation**, **commit/end/abort/event and drop semantics**, and **maximum length**. They cannot be certified from method names or mutation comments.
- **Randomness parsing and lock scope — no normal-path defect found under the stated path trust boundary.** `src/randomness.rs:15–28,49–69,105–122,139–158,166–169`. The mix is unambiguous with fixed-size prefixes and a final variable clock field. OS bytes use `read_exact`; state parsing reads up to byte 49, retries `Interrupted`, rejects over-reporting, bad magic and wrong length, and checks counter overflow before writing. `create_new` protects against replacing an existing entry including a dangling final symlink. `initialize` checks metadata on the opened handle and holds the lock on that handle through the update. It **does** follow links and can block waiting for a lock; it is not a safe hostile-path opener, nor does metadata checking undo effects of opening a special file. Cooperating callers must not replace the locked inode. The relaxed atomic checked increment suffices for unique counter allocation; a failed streaming signature consuming a draw is safe.
- **Secret retention — no evidence of a branch on raw signing-key bytes in BLINCS's Rust tree code.** `src/workspace.rs:12–30,47–57`; `src/few_time.rs:53–64`; `src/one_time.rs:115–135,203–215`; `src/pool.rs:27–40`; `src/lib.rs:188–201`. Retention is wider than “each worker”: participating application threads also keep scratch, jobs copy the secret key, and stack temporaries, batches and freed allocations are not securely erased. TLS lives for the **thread's** lifetime, not necessarily until process exit; allocator reuse is not erasure either. `Debug` for `SigningKey` and `Randomness` does not print their secret fields. Document the caller-thread and job copies and M1; do not infer a zeroization or side-channel guarantee for the external kernels.

All six review areas had at least one issue or material contract gap. The strongest positive result is the agreement of the **wire algorithms**. The most important next work is to make pool failure terminating and observable, correct the fork contract, establish the pinned runtime's thread and buffer guarantees, and separate the FORS probability calculation from the claimed security of the whole scheme.
